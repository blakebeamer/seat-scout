How the model works

Layout. Floor plus 100 (101–110, ~37 rows), 200 (201–211, 21 rows), 300 (301–311, 20 rows) and 400 (403–409, 25 rows), placed at their published angles off the stage centerline. Seats per row are modeled from published row maximums, so counts and seat numbering are close but not exact. Seat 1 is modeled on your left as you face the stage.

Scores. Each seat gets an estimated distance and height from the stage. Band view falls with distance, side angle and steep downward sightlines. Dome visuals peak in center 300 and mid 200, and drop hard under the 200 overhang. Vertigo risk blends height, rake, front-row exposure and screen motion. Comfort counts haptic seats (200–400 and the back half of 100), legroom for your height, stairs and aisles.

Prices. Face values are estimates anchored on Mayer's announced $129 floor and Dead & Company's all-in Sphere tiers ($145 / $195 / $295 / $395). Those standard prices line up with the Eagles' Sphere tiers ($175 / $225 / $325 / $425). The catch is Platinum: the best center blocks are mostly sold at dynamic prices, so by default those seats show a Platinum estimate (standard price × markup). Resale = standard price × heat × seat desirability × night demand.
